And somehow, if I get to trying it, I am often able to enjoy it. A side of me thinks my taste is pretty good because of this. I've never finished a book or videogame thinking that it wasn't worth it.

To tell the truth, I also happen to get recommendations from friends and reviewers with good taste, [excellent podcasts](https://intothecast.online), [YouTubers I trust](https://www.youtube.com/@bookpilled), and all the cool bloggers that I follow here, there is so much good stuff to enjoy, I rarely put myself in a position where I think of something as a slog.

I like to think I'm not easy to please though. I enjoy quality and the effort put into an artwork that goes above and beyond. I have experienced enough to differentiate between something made with passion or lack there of.

Even if the book is not particularly enjoyable, or if a mechanic in a game feels a little unfair, I am willing to let myself appreciate it. I'd even allow my brain to be fooled into thinking it's great. I will always try to focus on the intent behind any piece of media. To align my mindset with the artist's.

When I played [Resident Evil](/blog/resident-evil/) and [its sequel](/blog/resident-evil-2/) early this year, I wasn't thinking about the future beyond tank controls, I didn't worry about the scarce save points. I put myself in the shoes of someone who just bought a PS1 and wanted to try this neat game and it was how it was. Perfect experience. I didn't worry about outdated elements or cheesy voice acting, I lived in 1998 and everything was glorious.

As I read all of *The Expanse*, I wasn't expecting the beat space opera ever, or well explained hard sci-fi. I never thought I'd completely understand the cosmical anomalies present throughout the series. I enjoyed the characters and the bombastic action sequences. I let my brain shove away any magic technology or whatever and focused on the epic character moments and intrigue of it all.

While completing [*Metroid Prime Remastered*](/blog/metroid-prime-remastered/) it didn't even cross my mind to compare the 2D games. When someone said "this is just *Super Metroid* in 3D" I wasn't even considering *Super Metroid*'s existence. When that one area had no save points nearby I simply took it as the challenge it was, fair? unfair? It's a game, and I can go elsewhere to find more upgrades too. So, win?

After finishing [*Non-Stop*](/blog/non-stop/) by Brian W. Aldiss, I wasn't worried about the countless modern sci-fi books that have done generation ship plots better, I didn't mind the non-sensical biology of some of the world-building. I fully committed myself to the environment and its rules, and I had a wonderful time going through all of it. When annoying characters showed up, I am meant to be annoyed, and I push through that anyway.

If a game happens to have a lot of cutscenes, I push through. If I am told to backtrack for the 9th time to the same place, so be it. If a book lacks emotional moments and is more of a dry reporting of events, it is so. If the romance is outdated and cheesy, I still find it kinda cute.

This is pretty much the case for all the books and videogames and movies and pretty much any media I experience. I can pretend quality of life improvements don't exist, I can imagine myself in the worlds even if I despise the decisions or the politics or a particular character.

Every game I've played this year could be in someone's Top 5 of all time---maybe not the smaller titles/DLC that can be completed in an hour. Perhaps that can't be said of the books I've read this year, but they are still pretty good as well!

~~Now this is where I'll get challenged by a friend into reading through some boring stuff or playing a game that will make me suffer.~~

Even if a work is not as impactful for me as it may be for others, it's usually a 4/5 stars experience for me.

Perhaps I should be more critical, as my ranking system ends up kinda useless. Or maybe my standards are too low. Or I simply happen to choose excellent games and books.

Good thing I don't rank anything on my reviews.
