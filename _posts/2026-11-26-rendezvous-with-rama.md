Oh right I finished another book now. I am actually impressed by the amount of books I've read so far this year, and rather quickly too!

After completing [*Frankenstein*](/blog/frankenstein/) and [*Dune Messiah*](/blog/dune-messiah/), I wanted to try something more "futuristic" again. I just wanted some space, ships and aliens and all that presented in a way that felt more aligned with "hard science", so I thought this would do, and it delivered!

Another reason I wanted to give this a read, is that Denis Villeneuve, who directed the newest *Dune* movies, *Arrival* and *Bladerunner 2049*, is actually working on this film, and I wanted to see how this book would do.

In 2130, Humanity has conquered the solar system, and set up a "Spaceguard" to monitor asteroid activity and protect the United Planets from any danger from outer space---mostly giant rocks thus far. Now there are settlements in every non-gas planet---except for Venus---and a mostly normal society---chilling in our corner of the universe.

Until Rama showed up.

With a length of 50 km and a diameter of 20 km, the object is deemed too unnatural to be a mere asteroid. Scientists begin to study the characteristics of this stranger, which is named Rama, and they theorize that it may have been designed by intelligent beings, for unknown reasons.

It's quickly noted that if Humanity wants to explore it up close, there's only a window of a few weeks, before Rama gets too close to the sun, and eventually continues its trajectory beyond the solar system. A Comitee with scientists and people from all the planets is started as well, to continue working on understanding Rama and advicing the astronauts inside. They decide to send Commander Norton and his crew onboard the *Endeavour*, with a single mission: to *Rendezvous with Rama*...

(*insert epic Hans Zimmer music here*).

The *Endeavour*'s crew is a team of competent scientists, but none of them are true characters other than maybe Norton. We still get a few interesting moments though as they are used to represent multiple viewpoints of humanity.

There's an astronaut with religious beliefs, and he theorizes how Rama fits with his faith. There's a biologist obsessed with finding life and studying it at any cost. There's a rookie who wants to venture further than the others by rather dangerous means. It's not like you'll cry or feel any attachments to anyone here, I couldn't even bother to look up the names of these people. I did have some admiration though given the bravery of it all, but no big deal.

You really are here for the plot and the concepts. Clarke does a lot of very interesting world building, and slowly uncovers secret after secret, leaving us with crumbs and leads with few actual answers until the very end. There is some really good stuff when it comes to the way different human settlements react to Rama. Earth, Mercury, Mars and the like will share different ideas about how it may represent a menace or an opportunity, and there is some interesting political machinations that take place as well that will amp up the stakes of the whole plot.

When it comes to outdated elements... there are some for sure. There's a few sentences from Norton where he's like "women's bodies are too distracting in zero gravity". Nonetheless, there are some heroic moments performed by women as well, so that's cool. I always expect something like this to happen in old sci-fi books (unless they are by Mary Shelley or Le Guin, I guess) so it didn't catch me off-guard.

The chapters of this book are extremely short, less than ten pages each. I genuinely flew past them, reading both digitally and physically as it was convenient. I think this was a rather comfy read, even if the descriptions were a little overboard sometimes. I have to say I was *slightly* dissapointed by the finale when compared to what Clarke accomplished on [Childhood's End](/blog/childhoods-end/), which was a much later work. This is not as grandiose, but in a way, that's kind of what the novel is going for. It's definitely worth a read and I'm kinda curious about the rest of the Rama series. I have heard very mixed things about it though, and I know Clarke didn't write them himself, so yeah, I'm more than happy with the mystery we get on this one.
